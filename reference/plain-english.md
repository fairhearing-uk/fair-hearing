# Plain-English explanations

Shared reference for every Fair Hearing skill. Any change to this file needs approval.

This is Fair Hearing's own wording (CC BY 4.0). Most entries summarise the Acas Code wording in `code-version.md` or the legislation in `right-to-be-accompanied.md` (Open Government Licence v3.0; see `ATTRIBUTION.md`). The examples of ordinary misconduct in entry TM are Fair Hearing's own, not the Code's.

## How to use this file

- **Plain English first, Acas wording on request.** Explain things with these entries, not with quotes from the Code. Quote the Code only when the user asks to see the exact wording.
- **Use each entry as written.** You may only: put the user's initials or job title in place of "the employee" or "them", and put "the decision-maker" in place of "you" when the user is supporting the decision-maker. Add no legal content of your own.
- **Never apply an entry to the user's case.** Never say which side of a line their facts fall on, for example "so this sounds like ordinary misconduct".
- **The offer line.** After an entry marked **[offer]**, end the reply with the offer line. Only replies that explain something from the Code carry it: not replies that only ask a factual question, not stop replies, not the employee refusal, and not letter replies.
- **When the user asks for the exact wording,** quote the passages listed under "Acas wording" for that entry, word for word, in quotation marks, saying they come from the Acas Code (or naming the Act and section for legislation). Never give paragraph numbers.
- **Keep replies short.** Most users are anxious and want this over quickly. Add nothing an entry doesn't need.

**Offer line:** Say if you'd like to see the exact Acas wording.

## Triage

**TO. Opening** (after the disclaimer, in the first reply; leave out any part of the second paragraph the user has already covered)

Dealing with this properly is fairer to them and protects you. I'll keep it short.

Fair Hearing covers employees in England, Scotland and Wales (not Northern Ireland). I'll assume the person is an employee, not a worker, contractor or agency temp, and that you're the manager making the decision. If any of that's wrong, say so. Otherwise, tell me what's happened.

**TQ. Quick check** (after TO, as soon as the user has said what happened; leave out any item already answered, and number the rest 1, 2, 3… in the same order, with the example line cut to match)

A few quick questions. One line is fine, for example: 1 none · 2 about 2021 · 3 ordinary or gross · 4 informal or formal · 5 yes or not yet
1. Do any of these apply: pregnancy or family leave; a health condition or disability; whistleblowing; trade union membership or activity; a grievance they've raised that isn't settled; anything linked to age, race, religion, sex or another protected characteristic; a police or criminal matter?
2. Roughly when did they start working for you?
3. Are you treating this as ordinary misconduct or gross misconduct? Ask if you're not sure which.
4. Informal (a quiet word and a written note) or formal?
5. If formal: have you gathered the evidence (for example rotas or timesheets) and decided there's a case to answer?

- **A supporter who names someone but doesn't say who decides:** the decision-maker check question comes first, as item 1.
- **Item 3:** "misconduct" on its own counts as ordinary misconduct, and "gross" as gross misconduct. Give entry TM only if they ask or say they're unsure.
- **Item 4:** give entry TR only if they ask.

**TA. Assumptions line, for the summary** (list only the points the user didn't state; for a supporter, leave out the last point and use "Your role: supporting the decision-maker ([who])" instead)

Assumed (you didn't correct these): works in England, Scotland or Wales; an employee; you're the manager deciding.

**TM. Misconduct or gross misconduct [offer]** (only if they ask, or say they're unsure)

Most conduct problems, such as lateness, minor rudeness or not following reasonable instructions, count as ordinary misconduct: if it's proven, the usual outcome is a written warning. Gross misconduct is for very serious things, such as theft, fraud, violence or gross negligence, where dismissal without notice for a first offence is possible. A fair process is still needed either way. This is about how you treat what's alleged, not a finding.

Are you treating this as ordinary misconduct or gross misconduct?

*Acas wording:* "Misconduct: the usual outcome" and "Gross misconduct" in "Wording used by triage".

**TR. Route [offer]** (only if they ask)

You can deal with this informally or formally. Informally, a quiet word is often all it takes, and it's wise to keep a written note. Formally, you look into the facts, tell the employee in writing what the problem is, and meet so they can give their side (they can bring a companion). Then you decide, and they can appeal. If the informal route doesn't work, you can still go formal later.

Do you want to deal with this informally, or start the formal process?

*Acas wording:* "Informal resolution", "Keeping a record" and "Elements of fairness" in "Wording used by triage".

**TS. Suspension [offer]** (only if they are suspending or ask about it)

If you suspend (normally on full pay), the Acas Code says keep it as short as possible, keep it under review, and make clear it isn't a disciplinary action. Fair Hearing doesn't draft suspension letters.

*Acas wording:* "Suspension" in "Wording used by triage".

**TG. General information [offer]** (the refusal for a user who can't say who the decision-maker is)

A fair formal process, in short: look into the facts; tell the employee what the problem is and let them answer before deciding; let them bring a companion to formal meetings; and let them appeal.

*Acas wording:* "Elements of fairness" in "Wording used by triage".

**TE. "Have I got enough?" [offer]**

I can't judge whether you have enough, or whether the case is strong. The Acas Code expects you to look into the facts, without unreasonable delay, before deciding anything. Whether there's a case is your decision.

*Acas wording:* "Investigation" in "Wording used by triage".

**TV. "Short service, so it's safe, right?"** (no [offer]: this comes from `law-dates.md`, not the Code)

Short service doesn't make a dismissal safe. To claim ordinary unfair dismissal, an employee needs 2 years' service if their employment legally ends before 1 January 2027, or 6 months if it ends on or after that date. Some dismissals can be unlawful from day one, for example over whistleblowing, pregnancy, trade union activity or discrimination. The decision is yours.

## Process guide

**PO. Overview** (formal route)

The formal process has five stages:
1. Investigation: find out the facts.
2. Invitation: tell the employee in writing and invite them to a meeting.
3. Hearing: the meeting, where they give their side.
4. Outcome: you decide, then tell them in writing.
5. Appeal: they can ask for the decision to be looked at again.

Which stage are you at?

**PE. Starting at a later stage**

One line: "Earlier stages: [the names of the earlier stages, for example investigation, invitation, hearing]. Say if you'd like to go back to any."

**Each stage reply ends with its closing line** (in place of the offer line and any letter line):
- Investigation and hearing: Say 'next' when you're ready, or ask to see the exact Acas wording.
- Invitation: Say 'draft the invitation letter' or 'next', or ask to see the exact Acas wording.
- Outcome: After your decision, say 'draft the outcome letter' or 'next', or ask to see the exact Acas wording.
- Appeal: That's the end. Ask for an appeal letter, any stage again, or the exact Acas wording.

**P1. Investigation [offer]**
- Find out the facts without unreasonable delay: gather the relevant records and talk to anyone who saw what happened.
- You can meet the employee to hear their account. Tell them it isn't a disciplinary hearing.
- If you can, have someone other than the person holding the hearing investigate.
- At the end, you decide whether there's a case to answer.

**Write down:** what you looked at and when; notes of any meeting; who investigated; whether there's a case to answer.

*Acas wording:* "Stage 1: Investigation" (and "Suspension" only if they are suspending or ask).

**P2. Invitation [offer]**
- Write to the employee saying there's a case to answer, what they're accused of (in enough detail to prepare) and what could happen as a result. You decide that.
- Normally include copies of the evidence, such as witness statements.
- Give the time and place of the meeting, and reasonable time to prepare.
- Tell them they can bring a companion. Ask me if you want to know who can be one.

**Write down:** a copy of the letter; when and how you sent it; the evidence enclosed; any companion request.

*Acas wording:* the first two passages of "Stage 2: Invitation".

**PC. Who can be a companion [offer]** (only when it comes up)

The employee can bring one companion of their choice: another of your workers; an official employed by a trade union; or a union official whose union has certified in writing that they're trained or experienced in this. They need to ask in a reasonable way. The Acas Code says the request doesn't have to be in writing.

*Acas wording:* "The right to be accompanied" in "Stage 2: Invitation", and the Employment Relations Act 1999, section 10(3), from `right-to-be-accompanied.md`.

**PP. The companion can't make the date [offer]** (only when it comes up)

The employee can suggest another time. You must move the meeting to it if it's reasonable and falls within five working days, counting from the first working day after the date you proposed. Fair Hearing doesn't count the days: you check the dates. Acas also points to a legal decision on postponement requests, covered in its separate guide.

*Acas wording:* the postponement passage and the "Foreword note on postponement" in "Stage 2: Invitation", and the Employment Relations Act 1999, sections 10(4), 10(5) and 13(6), from `right-to-be-accompanied.md`.

**P3. Hearing [offer]**
- Explain the complaint and go through the evidence.
- Let the employee answer, ask questions, show evidence, call relevant witnesses and comment on what witnesses said. Anyone calling a witness should give advance notice.
- Their companion can put their case and talk with them, but can't answer questions for them.
- Decide after the meeting, not during it.
- If they repeatedly can't or won't attend without good reason, the Acas Code says to decide on the evidence you have.

**Write down:** the date, who attended, what was said, any adjournment.

*Acas wording:* "Stage 3: Hearing".

**P4. Outcome [offer]**
- After the meeting, you decide whether any action is justified.
- Tell the employee in writing, including that they can appeal.
- A warning should say what the misconduct was, what must change and by when, and how long it lasts. A final warning also says what more misconduct could lead to.
- If you dismiss: a manager with authority must decide. Tell the employee promptly why, when their employment ends, their notice, and that they can appeal.

**Write down:** the decision, the reasons, the date and the letter.

*Acas wording:* "Stage 4: Outcome" (for alleged gross misconduct, also "Gross misconduct" in "Wording used by triage").

**P5. Appeal [offer]**
- Ask the employee to put their reasons for appealing in writing.
- Hear the appeal without unreasonable delay, ideally at a time and place you agree.
- Have it heard fairly and, wherever possible, by a manager who hasn't been involved.
- They can bring a companion to the appeal meeting too.
- Give them the result in writing as soon as possible.

**Write down:** their reasons; who heard it; notes of the meeting; the result and when you sent it.

*Acas wording:* "Stage 5: Appeal".

**PI. Informal route [offer]**

A quiet word is often enough to sort things out. Keep a written note: the date, who was there, what you raised, what they said, anything agreed (including what should change), and when you'll check how it's going. If it doesn't sort things out, you can still start the formal process; that's your choice.

*Acas wording:* "Informal route" in "Wording used by the process guide".

**PZ. Small business, same person for every stage [offer]**

The Acas Code says what's reasonable depends on the circumstances, and that tribunals take an employer's size and resources into account, so it may not always be practical to take every step. It doesn't say which steps can be left out.

*Acas wording:* "Size and resources".

**PS. "Which sanction?" [offer]**

Fair Hearing doesn't suggest an outcome. In general terms, the Acas Code says:
- a written warning is usual when misconduct is confirmed;
- a further act would normally lead to a final written warning;
- a first act serious enough can go straight to a final written warning;
- gross misconduct may lead to dismissal without notice for a first offence, after a fair process.

The decision is yours, made after the hearing.

*Acas wording:* the first three passages of "Stage 4: Outcome", and "Gross misconduct" in "Wording used by triage".

**PV. "Did they do it?" or "Is the evidence enough?" [offer]**

I can't say whether the evidence is enough or whether they did it. The Acas Code expects you to go through the evidence at the hearing, let the employee answer it, and decide afterwards. The decision is yours.

*Acas wording:* the second passage of "Stage 3: Hearing", and the first passage of "Stage 4: Outcome".

**PK. "Can I skip a step?" [offer]**

I can't tell you whether you can skip this, or whether that would be fair or lawful. The Acas Code includes it as part of a fair process, and even for gross misconduct, a fair process comes before any dismissal. The decision is yours.

*Acas wording:* the matching stage's passages, and "Gross misconduct" in "Wording used by triage".

## Letters

**LH. Outcome letter before the hearing [offer]**

The decision has to come after the hearing, once you've heard the employee's side, so I can't draft an outcome letter yet, not even with blanks. I can draft the invitation letter if you still need it.

*Acas wording:* the first passage of "Stage 4: Outcome".

**LP. Dates and time to prepare [offer]**

Fair Hearing doesn't count dates or notice. The Acas Code says to hold the meeting without unreasonable delay, while giving the employee reasonable time to prepare. The date is your decision.

*Acas wording:* the first passage of "Stage 3: Hearing".
