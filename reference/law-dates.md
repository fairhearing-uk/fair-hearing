# Law dates: qualifying service for unfair dismissal

Shared reference for every Fair Hearing skill. Great Britain only (England, Wales and Scotland). All legal text below was checked on legislation.gov.uk on 15 November 2026.

## What Fair Hearing may say, and what it may not

**May say (as plain facts, not advice):**
- The qualifying period for ordinary unfair dismissal depends on the **effective date of termination**, which is usually the day employment legally ends:
  - **2 years** if the effective date of termination is **before 1 January 2027**;
  - **6 months** if it is **on or after 1 January 2027**. This applies even if the employee was dismissed before that date.
- Some dismissals can be automatically unfair **from day one**, with no qualifying service (see the list below). Discrimination protection under the Equality Act 2010 also has no qualifying-service condition. So short service never means a dismissal is safe.
- The legal limit on the compensatory award for unfair dismissal is removed from 1 January 2027.
- **Clearly past the threshold:** when the user's own figures show service clearly more than 2 years and 1 month, it may add this one sentence, word for word: "On what you've told me, their service is well past the qualifying period under either rule." This is outside the one-month borderline buffer under both rules, so no calculation is involved. It does not mean the dismissal is safe; the day-one exceptions below still apply. If the figures are within the buffer, unclear or not given, do not use the sentence: apply red flag 10 or ask.

**Must not:**
- calculate anyone's length of service or effective date of termination;
- say whether a particular employee is or isn't protected, except for the one permitted sentence above for service clearly more than 2 years and 1 month;
- give, estimate or compare any compensation figure;
- say a dismissal is "safe", "low risk" or similar.

**Borderline cases are red flags.** Three situations are covered by red flag 10 in the shared red-flags list, and the reply must be "take legal advice before going further":
- service within one month either side of the threshold;
- a case where the employment could end on or after 1 January 2027 when the decision is made before it;
- service between 6 months and 2 years with a possible end date in December 2026 or later, however the end date is phrased (transitional, like the one before it).

## The rules (source text)

**Employment Rights Act 1996, section 108(1)** (current text; extent England, Wales and Scotland):
> Section 94 does not apply to the dismissal of an employee unless he has been continuously employed for a period of not less than two years ending with the effective date of termination.

**Employment Rights Act 2025, section 25(2) and (3):**
> (2) In section 108 (qualifying period of employment)—
> (a) in subsection (1), for "two years" substitute "six months";
> …
> (3) Omit section 124 (limit of compensatory award etc).

**Employment Rights Act 2025 (Commencement No. 4 and Transitional and Saving Provisions) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/559), regulation 3:** section 25 comes into force on 1 January 2027 "to the extent not already in force".

**SI 2026/559, regulation 4(1):**
> The amendments made by section 25(2) and (3) of, and paragraphs 1(2), 1(3)(a), 1(4) to (7), 1(9), 2(3) and 4 of Schedule 3 to, the 2025 Act—
> (a) have effect in any case where an employee is dismissed before 1st January 2027 but the effective date of termination falls on or after that date;
> (b) do not have effect in any case where the effective date of termination falls before 1st January 2027.

## Statutory notice and the effective date of termination

**Employment Rights Act 1996, section 97(2):**
> Where—
> (a) the contract of employment is terminated by the employer, and
> (b) the notice required by section 86 to be given by an employer would, if duly given on the material date, expire on a date later than the effective date of termination (as defined by subsection (1)),
> for the purposes of sections 108(1), 119(1) and 227(3) the later date is the effective date of termination.

**Plain English:** if an employer dismisses without giving the statutory minimum notice, the end date used to count qualifying service can be pushed later, to when that notice would have ended. This is one reason Fair Hearing never calculates borderline service.

## Day-one exceptions: no qualifying service needed

Under **Employment Rights Act 1996, section 108(3) to (5)**, the qualifying period does not apply where the reason for dismissal falls under one of these. Headings are as shown on legislation.gov.uk.

- s.98B Jury service
- s.99 Leave for family reasons, including pregnancy and maternity
- s.100 Health and safety cases
- s.101 and s.101ZA Shop workers and betting workers who refuse Sunday work
- s.101A Working time cases
- s.102 Trustees of occupational pension schemes
- s.103 Employee representatives
- s.103A Protected disclosure (whistleblowing)
- s.104 Assertion of statutory right
- s.104A The national minimum wage
- s.104B Tax credits
- s.104C Flexible working
- s.104D Pension enrolment
- s.104E Study and training
- s.104F Blacklists
- s.104G Employee shareholder status
- s.105 Redundancy, where selection was for one of these reasons
- Rights under the Part-time Workers Regulations 2000, the Fixed-term Employees Regulations 2002 and the Agency Workers Regulations 2010
- Roles under various employee information and consultation regulations listed in s.108(3)
- s.108(4): the employee's political opinions or affiliation
- s.108(5): membership of a reserve force

**Trade union membership and activities:** **Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992, section 154** says the qualifying period does not apply to a dismissal that is unfair under section 152 or 153 (trade union membership and activities).

**Discrimination:** **Equality Act 2010, section 39(2)(c)** says an employer must not discriminate against an employee "by dismissing B". Section 39 sets no qualifying-service condition.

This list helps the skills recognise day-one risks. Fair Hearing does not decide whether any of these applies. The matching items in the red-flags list send the user to take legal advice.
