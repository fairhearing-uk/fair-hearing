# The statutory right to be accompanied

Shared reference for every Fair Hearing skill. Great Britain only (England, Wales and Scotland). This is the legislation itself, not the Acas Code, so it is **not** swapped when the Code changes. The Code's own wording on the right is in `code-version.md`.

Text quoted word for word from legislation.gov.uk, read directly on 15 November 2026. legislation.gov.uk showed "no known outstanding effects" for either section on that date. Editorial markers on the site (amendment brackets and reference letters such as F1 and M1) are left out; the words are unchanged. ## What Fair Hearing may say, and what it may not

**May say (as plain facts, not advice):**
- the right applies to a disciplinary hearing, including an appeal hearing, as defined below;
- who can be a companion under the right;
- that the request must be reasonable;
- the postponement rule: the worker proposes an alternative time, which must be reasonable and fall within the five-working-day period set out below, and what counts as a working day;
- what the companion may and may not do at the hearing.

**Plain English first.** Explain the right with entries PC and PP in `plain-english.md`. Quote the legislation below, word for word, naming the Act and section, only when the user asks to see the exact wording. When they ask about postponement, also quote the Code's postponement wording and the Foreword's note on postponement requests from `code-version.md`.

**Must not:**
- count working days, or say whether a particular date is inside or outside the five-working-day period. Give the rule and the definition and say the user checks the dates;
- decide whether a particular request is reasonable;
- say whether refusing a companion outside the statutory categories, or refusing a postponement, would be fair or unfair;
- treat the choice of a trade union official or representative as a companion as a red flag on its own (see flag 4 in `red-flags.md`).

## Employment Relations Act 1999, section 10 (extent England, Wales and Scotland)

**Section 10(1):**
> (1) This section applies where a worker—
> (a) is required or invited by his employer to attend a disciplinary or grievance hearing, and
> (b) reasonably requests to be accompanied at the hearing.

**Section 10(2A) to (2C):**
> (2A) Where this section applies, the employer must permit the worker to be accompanied at the hearing by one companion who—
> (a) is chosen by the worker; and
> (b) is within subsection (3).
>
> (2B) The employer must permit the worker's companion to—
> (a) address the hearing in order to do any or all of the following—
> (i) put the worker's case;
> (ii) sum up that case;
> (iii) respond on the worker's behalf to any view expressed at the hearing;
> (b) confer with the worker during the hearing.
>
> (2C) Subsection (2B) does not require the employer to permit the worker's companion to—
> (a) answer questions on behalf of the worker;
> (b) address the hearing if the worker indicates at it that he does not wish his companion to do so; or
> (c) use the powers conferred by that subsection in a way that prevents the employer from explaining his case or prevents any other person at the hearing from making his contribution to it.

**Section 10(3), who can be a companion:**
> (3) A person is within this subsection if he is—
> (a) employed by a trade union of which he is an official within the meaning of sections 1 and 119 of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992,
> (b) an official of a trade union (within that meaning) whom the union has reasonably certified in writing as having experience of, or as having received training in, acting as a worker's companion at disciplinary or grievance hearings, or
> (c) another of the employer's workers.

**Section 10(4) and (5), postponement:**
> (4) If—
> (a) a worker has a right under this section to be accompanied at a hearing,
> (b) his chosen companion will not be available at the time proposed for the hearing by the employer, and
> (c) the worker proposes an alternative time which satisfies subsection (5),
> the employer must postpone the hearing to the time proposed by the worker.
>
> (5) An alternative time must—
> (a) be reasonable, and
> (b) fall before the end of the period of five working days beginning with the first working day after the day proposed by the employer.

**Section 10(6), time off for a fellow worker who accompanies:**
> (6) An employer shall permit a worker to take time off during working hours for the purpose of accompanying another of the employer's workers in accordance with a request under subsection (1)(b).

## Employment Relations Act 1999, section 13 (extent England, Wales and Scotland)

**Section 13(4), what counts as a disciplinary hearing:**
> (4) For the purposes of section 10 a disciplinary hearing is a hearing which could result in—
> (a) the administration of a formal warning to a worker by his employer,
> (b) the taking of some other action in respect of a worker by his employer, or
> (c) the confirmation of a warning issued or some other action taken.

**Section 13(6), what counts as a working day:**
> (6) For the purposes of section 10(5)(b) in its application to a part of Great Britain a working day is a day other than—
> (a) a Saturday or a Sunday,
> (b) Christmas Day or Good Friday, or
> (c) a day which is a bank holiday under the Banking and Financial Dealings Act 1971 in that part of Great Britain.

## Letter clause (used by the `draft-letters` skill)

The companion sentence for every letter that mentions a companion: letter 1 (invitation, warning possible), letter 2 (invitation, dismissal possible) and letter 5 (appeal invitation). It is Acas's own wording from its disciplinary hearing letter templates (see `ATTRIBUTION.md`). The template marks it as `{{COMPANION}}`. Copy it exactly; fill only the notice the user has set.

```
You are entitled to be accompanied by someone you work with or a trade union representative. If you would like to be accompanied, please let me know the name of your chosen companion [number of hours or days] before the hearing.
```

- **Fair Hearing's choice:** this wording, the same in all three letter types, for consistency. In Fair Hearing's view, "trade union representative" is the accepted wording in employment relations and covers union officials.
- **No postponement wording in any letter.** The postponement rule above is for explaining to the user, not for the letter.
- Never use this sentence to decide who the employee may bring. For that question, use entry PC in `plain-english.md` (section 10(3) above, on request).

**Plain English:** the right covers hearings that could lead to a formal warning or other action, and appeal hearings. For investigatory meetings, use the Code's own wording in `code-version.md` (Stage 1), which says there is no statutory right to be accompanied at a formal investigatory meeting.
